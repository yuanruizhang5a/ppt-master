# Basics: Source Understanding and Coverage

Source: `projects/course-rocq/lf/Basics.html`; visual reference: `projects/course-rocq/output_R1.pdf`.

## Teaching Summary

Gallina treats pure programs as mathematical functions and functions as first-class values. Inductive declarations describe exactly how values can be constructed. Pattern matching consumes those values; structural recursion makes recursive computation terminate. Program properties are propositions, and proof tactics construct checked evidence. Students progress from concrete evaluation to general proofs, then combine modeling, computation, and proofs in realistic examples.

## Essential Content

1. **Functional programming and the workflow.** Pure functions, first-class functions, algebraic data types, pattern matching, Gallina versus tactics; `Compute`, named `Example` proofs, and extraction to OCaml/Haskell/Scheme. Extraction relies on the extraction implementation and target compiler. `From Stdlib Require Export String.` is used for course tooling.
2. **Enumerated types and days.** `Inductive day`, constructors, `Definition next_working_day`, complete matches, explicit argument/result types and type inference. Friday maps to Monday; two working days after Saturday is Tuesday.
3. **Booleans.** `bool`, `negb`, `andb`, `orb`, complete truth-table examples, multiargument application, `&&`/`||` notation, and equivalent conditionals. A conditional can inspect any inductive type with two constructors, selecting the first or second constructor branch. `nandb` and `andb3` are practice opportunities.
4. **Types and structured values.** `Check` versus `Compute`; arrow types and functions as values; `rgb`/`color` and parameterized constructors; valid versus ill-typed constructor expressions; ordered, nested and wildcard patterns. Modules isolate names. A single constructor with multiple fields represents tuples (`bit`, `nybble`, `bits`, `all_zero`).
5. **Natural numbers and computation.** Unary `nat` with `O` and `S`, finite expressions representing an infinite set; constructor names are representation choices. Constructors construct data, whereas defined functions reduce. Decimal notation, `pred`, `minustwo`, recursive `even` and defined `odd`; `Fixpoint` for structural recursion. `plus`, its evaluation trace, `mult`, saturating `minus`, `exp`, and factorial. Natural subtraction stops at zero.
6. **Comparisons and notation.** Recursive `eqb`/`leb`, deriving `ltb`; `=?`, `<=?`, `<?` compute booleans while `=` states a proposition. Notation changes parsing/printing, not definitions. Lower precedence numbers bind more tightly; multiplication level 40 precedes addition level 50, comparison level 70; associativity and scopes (`nat_scope`, `%nat`, `%Z`).
7. **Simplification proofs.** `Proof`/`Qed`, `Example`/`Theorem`/`Lemma` as naming conventions, universal quantification, context and goal, `intros`, `simpl`, `reflexivity`. `reflexivity` itself computes/unfolds to check convertibility. Left-zero addition reduces by definition; an unknown matched argument can block computation.
8. **Rewriting proofs.** Implication and hypotheses; `intros n m H`; rewriting forward/backward; rewriting with existing universally quantified theorems; `plus_id_example`, chained equalities, and the orientation of `mult_n_O`/`mult_n_Sm`. `Admitted` leaves an assumption; it is not a checked proof. `Abort` abandons a declaration.
9. **Case analysis.** `destruct n as [|n'] eqn:E`, generated subgoals, retained equations, bullets, nested bullets and braces. Explain why `(n + 1) =? 0` is stuck and how exposing either constructor resolves it. Boolean negation involution, conjunction commutativity, simplifying hypotheses (`simpl in H`), rewriting contradictions, and introduction patterns (`intros [|n]`, `intros [] []`). Case analysis supplies cases without an induction hypothesis.
10. **Higher-order practice.** Identity/negation functions applied twice and the optional conjunction/disjunction equality exercise connect function-valued variables with rewriting and cases.
11. **Formalized grading policy.** Letters A/B/C/D/F, Plus/Natural/Minus, `Grade`, three-valued `comparison`, lexicographic grade comparison. Pattern alternatives abbreviate cases. `lower_letter F = F` refutes unconditional strict lowering; strict lowering needs a precondition. `lower_grade` progresses through modifiers and crosses letter boundaries, saturating at F-. Lowering F- remains F-. Late-day bands are 0–8 / 9–16 / 17–20 / >=21 with zero / one / two / three steps. Unfold-via-equality and rewriting assumptions prove the first two policy branches. This is the chapter's example policy, not a claim about the instructor's actual rules.
12. **Binary numerals.** `bin := Z | B0 bin | B1 bin`, least significant bit outermost (left in printed constructor syntax); numeric interpretations are 0, twice the inner value, and twice the inner value plus 1. `B0 Z` also denotes zero. `incr` propagates carry through B1; `bin_to_nat` recurses over the representation. Examples 1→2 and 3→4 show carry; computation tests do not prove a general correctness claim.
13. **Homework and trust.** Preserve exercise names/statements, retain unfinished declarations with `Admitted` rather than `Abort`, track admitted helper dependencies, compile Basics before BasicsTest with `-Q . LF`, and interpret type checks and assumptions separately. “Closed under the global context” indicates no unproved assumptions in that check; an allowed axiom is a course-specific exception. Manual grading remains separate.
